# Thesis

## The organisation is the system

Most organisational software is built around applications and records: a CRM has contacts, a project tool has tasks, a document system has files, and an inbox has messages.

The organisation itself does not work like that. It is a changing web of people, relationships, observations, intentions, decisions, commitments, practices, evidence and actions, unfolding through time.

An organisational operating system is therefore not a giant application. It is the shared language and infrastructure that lets different tools recognise that they are looking at different parts of the same organisation.

## Keep the thread

Organisations already notice, remember, decide, promise, act and learn. The problem is that the thread between those things is scattered across people, applications, documents and time.

The aim is not to put everything in one place. It is to let each thing remain where it is useful, while making the relationships between them durable enough for the organisation — and the people and agents working within it — to understand what is happening and act coherently.

## The active cycle

NOTICE → DECIDE → ACT → LEARN → ADAPT ↺

This is not a prescribed workflow. Real organisational activity moves backwards, sideways and around the loop.

Four capabilities cut across the cycle:

- **Relationships** give organisational state shape.
- **Memory / time** keeps the thread intact.
- **Attention** determines what matters here and now.
- **Provenance** tells us why we should trust what we think we know.

## Products are lenses

Products remain distinct because of the question they help the organisation answer, not because they own a private version of organisational reality.

- Swells — what are we noticing?
- Tending — what needs tending?
- Attention — what deserves attention now?
- TOPO — what do we know, and how did we get here?
- FlowLance — what are we doing?
- RACK — how do we work?
- Glade — what matters, and how are we doing?
- Drift — where are reality and intention diverging?

## What belongs to the organisation?

A useful design question is:

> What belongs to the organisation, rather than to the application that happened to record it?

A relationship does not stop being a relationship when it leaves a CRM. A decision does not stop being a decision because it happened in email. An observation does not become less important because it was recorded outside a project system.

## Success

The project succeeds when another application can participate meaningfully without copying our software or understanding our internal databases.

If the specification only describes Good Ship internal APIs, it is an integration layer rather than an organisational operating system.
